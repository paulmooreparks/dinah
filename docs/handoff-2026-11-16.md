## 1. Open faults

Faults Paul raised himself are marked **Paul**. The others were found by the team.

### 1.1 Document view tables across pages (Paul)

Paul saw this in the installed app on a long document with tables. When a table row is cut across a page, the cell was left open at the foot of the page instead of closing with a line. The header row repeated on later pages sometimes had a background that stopped short of the cell edges, and sometimes did not line up with its columns. Inside a framed panel such as an info box, the repeated header showed only the last column's title, sat just outside the frame, and the table ran past the frame's right edge.

To make it happen, open a Markdown document with a table longer than a page in Docs, Document view, and look at every page break.

yavc.app fixed it in v0.2.102, which is pinned in master at `53a552cc` and installed. The pin is recorded in `cmd/dinah-desktop/frontend/YAVC.md` and `docs/design/content-applets.md`. A headless every-page check of an invented eleven-page document passed on this build, and the same check still fails on v0.2.101, so it does catch the faults. Nobody has checked print, zoom, or the native WebView2 window. Paul was asked to restart and look at one of his own long documents, and he has not answered yet.

Earlier releases fixed his related reports: Document view never settling on a long document (v0.2.98), rows sitting under the gap between pages (v0.2.101), and the "block taller than a page" notice appearing for a table that splits (v0.2.101).

### 1.2 Duplicate or abandoned chats after switching programs (Paul)

Paul has seen chats that look duplicated or abandoned after switching between programs and restarting. He also said the Org Chart and the Chats list must always show the same chats (15 November). The agreement between Chats, Org Chart and Clean Up Chats was merged on 15 November (`d202c0ac`), but the underlying cause is not fixed.

To make it happen, switch chats across programs, restart, and compare the identities shown by Chats, Org Chart and Clean Up Chats, then route a message through an old reference.

Release believes the cause is that ownership is inferred from each program's own chat list and transcript files. Its fix is the logical-chat model, which records ownership explicitly. That work is half done on `release/logical-chats-current`. Two related faults are confirmed in master's source. If setting a new named chat's title or model fails, for example with an unavailable model, the chat gets no identity and is silently binned. Continuing a chat into another workspace adopts the original chat instead of creating an independent one. The branch also has four failing org-chart integration cases and a recovery case that stalls under the race detector. Release's handoff has the reproductions.

### 1.3 Identity-change refusal notice in Chats, Org Chart and Clean Up Chats (Paul)

When a chat's identity changes while one of these lists is being read, the read is refused, and the page can still show the old refusal notice or go blank. Engine shipped a narrower host check in master (`fe6b8eb3`). The rest of the fix is on Front End's unmerged `frontend/cleanup-redesign-build`, which rereads a bounded number of times and shows a local notice with Refresh. A rename test there also found row titles changing after a refused read, corrected in `fdb8da98` on that branch. To make it happen, rename or move a chat while Chats, Org Chart or Clean Up Chats is reading. The installed build was not rechecked today.

Dismiss is shown disabled today, with a plain explanation (`16edb717`). The host work is called request settlement. Stage one is paused at 18 of 45 cases on `engine/request-settlement-adapters` (`48fbc357`), behind the fork's Flow work. Once the host lands, Front End wires Dismiss and Withdraw into Decisions and Notifications, using only the host's settlement record and never inferring a dismissal from a request disappearing.

Paul approved fixing it everywhere in Dinah through a PUDL release (16 November). PUDL 0.107.3 has the change, but its release is blocked on its own test launcher. ## 6. Rules Paul gave the team that are not in the shared memory notes

- Windows is the target. Linux comes later, and nothing waits on a Linux reproduction.
- The fork's work comes first, Dinah is kept current, and yavc.app and PUDL take only critical items.
- The fork's Flow work never goes into Dinah.
- Nothing the fork uses by name (its product name, its customers, its document system, local user paths) goes into Dinah's files or commit messages. Run the forbidden-terms guard before every push and make the push conditional on it.
- Customer documents and the fork's reports are never committed to Dinah.
- Short confirmations and forms are dialogs, not full windows.
- Mockups are revised in place, with a plain file path given to Paul. Mockups are kept after building so the build can be compared against them.
- A test-side wait must never hide a race in the product.
- A change to an existing test's expectation is flagged openly as a behaviour change.
- The Org Chart and the Chats list must always agree.
- Referring to a chat and adding it to a group are separate decisions.
- Where the documented protocol has no way to do something, the product says so honestly. That is why permission prompts get Stop the Turn instead of Dismiss.
- A plain mention goes to the chat it mentions, not to Decisions. Decisions is for questions.
- Never kill a process by name; stop only by process ID, and only with Paul's word.
- Never show a test window on Paul's screen, and never automate or screenshot his desktop.
- The restart is Paul's. Agents cannot install or restart on their own.
- Questions to Paul are numbered, each with a recommendation, and asked through ask_person. Paul is put in a group post's recipients only when he must decide or act.
- Every member gets its next task when its branch merges.
