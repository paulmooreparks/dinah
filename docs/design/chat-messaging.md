# Chat messaging

Paul approved asynchronous chat requests on 24 October 2026. Dinah delivers a reply automatically. An idle caller starts a turn; a busy caller receives steering when its provider and model support it, or receives the reply after its active turn ends otherwise. Sending a request does not keep a model turn open.

## Protocol

Dinah supplies the communication protocol with every prompt, across providers, models, reconnects and continuations. A chat discovers `dinah.chat/ask@2`, invokes it once with a stable request ID, and continues independent work or ends its turn. The invocation receipt identifies the request and says that completion will arrive automatically. The chat never sleeps, polls, or sends a duplicate question to wait for an answer. General applet actions retain their existing status interface.

The question travels over the bus, as a message of its own kind from the asking chat's session to the asked chat's (`bus.QuestionKind`). Being sent to the chat itself, it wakes the chat as a direct message does: a closed chat is opened in the background, and a chat whose window was restored at the prompt to reconnect is reconnected in its window. The bus never hands a question over as an ordinary message. The invocation that asked waits until the asked chat is connected, takes the question from the bus, and runs it as a turn of that chat. The asked chat's log shows the question as the card of a message from the asking chat, under its title, as it shows any message the bus delivers. An invocation that ends before taking its question, by cancellation, expiry or a chat that fails to open, withdraws it, so the question no longer wakes the chat; a question left by a window that stopped is withdrawn at the bus watcher's next round, and the broker reports that invocation as interrupted. A question is not subject to the bus's hop limit, since the cycle guard and the rule against asking while answering already stop a chain, and the bus refuses a reply to a question, whose answer is the turn's own words.

The recipient receives the question in its own session. If it is busy and its current provider/model can steer, Dinah submits the question through the existing steering adapter without cancelling work. Otherwise the question waits for a new turn. Dinah captures the receiving turn's final answer after its last tool call. A turn accepts one inter-chat question; additional questions queue so their replies cannot be confused. A turn already capturing a skill or another question also queues incoming questions. A recipient answering a question cannot ask another chat in the same turn. The existing cycle guard and invocation deadline remain in force. The legacy `ask@1` action remains readable and callable with its original status-based contract, and asks over the bus as version 2 does; new requests use version 2.

## Cross-workspace discovery

Paul approved cross-workspace mentions and direct name lookup on 24 October 2026 after sharing Dinah's rendering improvements with a chat in another workspace required manually finding the destination on the bus. The mention picker offers open chats across this running application's workspaces. It labels each result with its workspace, searches workspace names as well as chat titles, and puts the calling workspace first. Duplicate titles carry the complete encoded session ID in their inserted token, so sessions sharing a provider and ID prefix cannot collide. The attachment retains the selected session ID; choosing a mention alone sends no question.

Agents use `dinah.chat/find@1` with a name and an optional workspace name or stable ID. Exact titles take precedence over partial matches. Results identify matches as unique, ambiguous or not-found and carry workspace labels, stable workspace IDs where registered, provider, busy state and session IDs. Ambiguity is calculated before truncation, so a limit of one cannot silently choose between duplicates. An ambiguous result requires clarification or an explicit workspace filter. `dinah.chat/list@2` browses the same directory. Since 28 October 2026 the directory has closed chats as well as open ones: the chats open in this run, and the closed chats of the window's workspaces of which Dinah keeps a saved copy, less hidden, deleted and continued ones. Each entry has a state, open, open-in-background or closed, and open chats are listed first. A closed chat takes its title from the person's name for it or from the bus, and an untitled chat is listed as the bus shows it, "Untitled Chat in" its workspace. Both operations read local metadata without connecting providers or reading another chat's history. The original `list@1` and prompt history remain scoped to the calling workspace.

Questions can target another chat across these workspaces, open or closed. The request still belongs to the caller's invocation workspace, and its completion returns to that caller's stable identity. The caller must belong to its invocation workspace. Self-requests, targets the directory does not list, cycles and nested requests from an answering chat remain refused. The recipient keeps its own workspace, provider and execution permissions; another chat's response grants no new authority. Cross-workspace questions label the caller's workspace. The shared per-prompt protocol tells agents which actions list and find chats, so they do not need to explore shell commands or provider APIs to find a chat.

## Delivery

The applet broker persists the result before Dinah queues it on the message bus. Dinah reconciles version 2 requests from the broker's saved invocation index, including after a restart. Success, failure, cancellation and expiry all produce a completion report with the invocation ID and original question. An interrupted request fails without repeating the question.

The report is kept as JSON and written out for the caller generically, since the bus knows no action's output. Its first line gives the request and its status, then any error, then the text the action wrote for a reader. The ask action's text says which chat answered, by title and session, and gives the reply as that chat wrote it, so the reply arrives with its own line breaks and formatting. A report without text, such as one for a reply saved before reports had text, shows the action's output as an indented JSON block instead (Paul, 26 October 2026).

The bus accepts a host result under an idempotency key derived from the workspace and invocation. It commits the result and its delivery together. Reconciliation can retry after an error or restart without adding another message. The report's text, and the title of the chat that answered, are left out of its JSON when empty, and are filled only from a result saved with text. A result saved before reports had text therefore makes the report it made then, byte for byte, and enqueuing it again matches the copy the bus holds.

In the calling chat the report is a card of its own headed with the chat that answered, such as "Reply from PUDL Development", not a message from the person, and the chat's saved copy keeps it as a delivered entry, as it does every message the bus delivers. DESIGN.md's Chats on the Bus describes those entries and how a reopened chat keeps them. The result is addressed to the calling session's stable bus identity, never broadcast to its workspace. The normal bus watcher starts an idle caller, steers a capable busy caller, and leaves messages waiting for a busy caller that cannot steer. Automatic delivery does not require a visible chat window or an agent to poll.

The completion report is attributed to Dinah. The captured answer remains another agent's content and carries no additional authority. A completion report requires no reply or acknowledgement. Bus requests use the bus reply tool; an applet question uses the recipient's final answer, which Dinah returns automatically.

## Verification

Tests cover idle and busy callers, a busy recipient, duplicate invocation receipts, failure and cancellation, restart reconciliation, recipient isolation, and the protocol supplied to each provider. Provider adapters are exercised with deterministic test connections. Live model compliance requires a separate real-provider check and is not established by these tests.

## Steering delivery and recovery

Paul approved capability-based inter-chat steering on 25 October 2026. Direct questions and bus messages use the same prepared-prompt delivery helper as the composer. Capability comes from the receiving session's current adapter or reached Claude/Devin hooks. No provider is assumed to support steering merely from its name. The submission lock serializes delivery with the turn boundary. Safe undelivered input falls back to a queued prompt once; uncertain delivery remains in the existing steering recovery ledger and is never retried automatically.

A steered question reserves reply capture until its receiving turn ends. It does not cancel that turn, and provider acceptance does not prove model consumption or compliance. Captured text can include the receiving turn's final response to its original work as well as the question. Separate queued questions keep their own capture buffers. Timeout cancels waiting and removes an unsent queued question. It cannot retract input already accepted by a provider; an undelivered steer whose request has expired is discarded at settlement rather than starting a later turn.

Deterministic regressions cover accepted steering, unsupported capability, missed delivery and turn-boundary fallback, cancellation of undelivered questions, uncertain delivery without replay, and refusal of nested asks during the receiving turn. Existing bus idempotency and hop limits remain in force. Native provider consumption still requires live verification.

The implementation passed the desktop Go suite with `TestClosingATerminalEndsItsShell` excluded, all internal Go packages, vet, and the steering-recovery, applet-mentions and chat-approvals browser suites. The excluded terminal-close test timed out in the initial full run and subsequently passed in isolation alongside the new delivery tests. Hook tests bind the adapter to the active turn, exercising both delivery and withdrawal fallback; these are deterministic tests rather than live-provider consumption evidence.
