The twelve-character internal identifier leads the line in two places: the columns of a listing, and the one-line confirmation a command prints after it acts. It is the position that tells a reader to start there, and it is the one value on the line nobody types and nobody acts on. The operator has said repeatedly that he almost never wants to see it, and the table work has already dropped it from one listing on his ruling. The confirmation line is the second instance and the more telling one: with the identifier gone it reads as the card's title and where the card now stands, which needs no further explanation, and with it present a reader is met by twelve characters of hex before anything meaningful. The identifier is still needed occasionally, so the card is about asking for it rather than choosing between always and never, and no way of asking exists today.
