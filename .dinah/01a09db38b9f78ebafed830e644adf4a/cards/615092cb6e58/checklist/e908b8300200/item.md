A nested archive restores in two acts, the read that cannot reach the inner entity says so in the read sense and points at the holder, and nothing is lost in between. TestANestedArchiveRestoresInTwoActs in cmd/dinah/restore_test.go archives a comment, then archives the card that holds it, then asserts four things. First, `dinah show --archived <card>/comments/1` refuses dinah.not-archived: the head is not the reference's deepest collection step so it resolves live and fails, and section 4.1's refusal probe then finds the comment by reading the mirror at the head. That stderr carries the read-sense base sentence, matched on the fragment `nothing in the archive answers to`, and a next-step clause naming `dinah restore <card>`; it carries neither the restore-sense fragment `nothing to restore` nor any clause naming a collection listing. Second, `dinah restore <card>` exits 0 and the restored card directory still carries its own archive/comments directory holding the comment's identifier. Third, `dinah restore <card>/comments/1` then exits 0. Fourth, `dinah show <card>/comments` finally lists both comments with the restored one's body byte-identical to what was written before either archive. The comment's body is captured before the first archive and compared at the end, so a restore that returns an empty directory fails rather than passing on the directory's existence alone.
