note: "Three alternatives were weighed and all three lose. A PER-STEP HALF is reference syntax rather than a flag, and dinah-456 section 8 refuses that class of widening. A LIVE-THEN-ARCHIVE FALLBACK at every step is defeated by positions: with one live comment and one archived comment, `pb-1/comments/1` would resolve live and the archived one would be unreachable, which is the whole condition the flag exists for. A MIRROR-FIRST FALLBACK at every step, raised by Agent Design Review in round 1 and absent from the first version of this note, survives both of those tests: it gives the same answer the chosen rule gives for `pb-1/comments/1` with one live and one archived comment, and it additionally reaches the entity inside an archived holder that the chosen rule refuses. It loses on this decision's own ground rather than on a new one. Under mirror-first the answer at the deepest step depends on what happens to be archived at a step above it, so archiving an unrelated holder silently changes what an existing address resolves to, which is the same defect the live-then-archive reading is refused for, one level up. It is also a half-per-step rule wearing a single flag, which is the grammar section 8 refuses, so it buys the reach of a per-step half without declaring itself as one. The cost of the rule chosen is that an entity sitting inside an archived holder has no address under the flag, and section 3 states that limit by name and gives the route, which is to restore the holder. Section 4.1's refusal probe reads live first and then the mirror, which is neither of the rejected readings: it produces no answer, only which of two refusal names to print, and live first there makes the live case win any tie so the advice never sends a reader to restore something already standing in front of them. Verified at 9260a2a that the mirror is local to its holder, so the two-act restore composes with no extra machinery."
