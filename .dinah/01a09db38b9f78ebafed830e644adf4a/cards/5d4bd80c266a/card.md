Dinah gains a way for a workbench to hold a card at a step until a named item is settled. The operator ruled on 2026-09-09, on dinah-449, that this is built before this project's development moves onto Dinah.

**The question this card was originally filed with is answered, so do not reopen it.** It was filed asking whether to build the mechanism at all, on the grounds that Dinah's existing rule might be enough and that a per-step hold buys precision at the cost of somewhere new to place things wrongly. That went to the operator with both alternatives and he chose to build. What remains is the design and the code, not the decision.

**What is true today**, established by reading the trunk on 2026-09-08 and worth re-deriving before anything is designed, since the trunk has moved since. An item's frontmatter can carry a `column`, and the write side stores it, but nothing enforces it: the field is read only to render a column's title. The single enforcement anywhere is that a card carrying an unanswered question or decision cannot be claimed or pulled. That refusal does not know which column the card sits in, does not fire on a move, and excludes acceptance criteria by a decision compiled into the tool with a comment saying so, because a criterion is verified after the work rather than before it.

**The shape the ruling settled.** A workbench declares which of its own steps refuse a card while a named item naming that step is unsettled. No kind of item is special-cased in the tool. A workbench that wants its acceptance criteria held says so in its own definition; one that wants nothing held says nothing; and neither answer is compiled in. That constraint came from the operator directly: Dinah has to stay usable for workbenches with no code, no merge and no tests, so this board's vocabulary must not reach the core. A design that names a step in Go rather than in workbench data fails the ruling however well it works here.

**Prior art in this repository, described accurately rather than flatteringly.** `docs/spec/core-profile.md` declares CORE-GATE-1 and CORE-GATE-2, which state the same mechanism: a workbench may require a named item resolved before a column entry, with no item kind excluded. Two rounds of review established both that the statements exist and that calling them ratified overstates it, because the document sits on a maturity channel whose own rules say nothing below it binds, and the conformance tests confirm neither statement is implemented. So the design is not invented from nothing and is not agreed either. Read both statements before designing, and say in the spec whether this card implements them as written or departs from them, because a card that quietly diverges from its own project's draft leaves two contradictory descriptions of one mechanism.

**What the design has to answer.** Whether the hold refuses entry to the step it names or departure from the one before it, since the two read alike in prose and differently in code, and this board had that backwards in writing within the last two days. Where the declaration lives in a workbench's own data, and what a workbench that declares nothing does. Whether the refusal names the item or only a count. Whether a hold can be overridden, by whom, and whether the override is recorded. And what happens to a hold naming a step that a particular card never reaches, which on a single-route board cannot arise and would arise the moment routes are added.

**A trap to design against rather than document around.** An item that assigns work to a step must not hold that step, or the card can never reach the place the work happens. That has bitten the Andoneer board repeatedly, always after the rule was written down, which is the argument for the tool refusing it rather than an instruction asking people not to do it.

**Scope kept out.** Routes are not part of this card: lanes stay filed and deferred, and the single-route board is what lets this mechanism stay simple. Nothing here asks for a board UI, which the product framing rules out.

## Specification

## Re-derivation, against trunk `b825059` (`git -C <worktree> log -1`, fetched fresh into this card's own worktree)

**What enforces what today.** `internal/verb/checks.go`'s `checkLists[Claim]` carries `UnresolvedItem` as `check.claim.7` (CORE-CLAIM-9); `checkLists[Move]` (nine rows, `check.move.1`-`.9`, the ninth Dinah's own loop-limit row) carries no such row at all, confirming the description's claim that the refusal "does not fire on a move." `claimableItems` (`internal/verb/mutate.go:157-172`) calls `bench.BlockingItems` -> `bench.ItemBlocksClaim` (`internal/bench/entity.go:914-936`), whose own comment states the exemption: `"An acceptance criterion never blocks: it is verified after the work rather than before it."` The refusal names the first blocking item's own ID (`mutate.go:172`, `blocking[0].ID`), not a count.

**The field already there.** `internal/bench/item.go:19-22`, `ItemColumnField = "column"`, comment: *"written only when a caller supplies one. Nothing reads it for enforcement today."* `internal/verb/read.go:862-869` reads it only to resolve a column title for display. `bench.AddItem` (`item.go:81-96`) already stores it. No new field is needed; this card's whole build is enforcement on top of a field that already round-trips.

**How an item gets resolved.** `internal/verb/checklist.go`'s `File`, `Cite`, `Resolve`, `Verify`, `Fail`, `Reopen` are, by that file's own header comment, *"comment-shaped acts rather than claim-shaped ones"*: none of the six touches the claim system, none requires the caller hold the card, and none reads or checks the card's column. Anybody can resolve any item on any card at any time regardless of where the card stands. This matters for the "wrong-gate" design question below.

**Prior art, read in full.** `docs/spec/core-profile.md` §5.9 (`CORE-ITEM-1..3`) and §6.4 (`CORE-GATE-1`, `CORE-GATE-2`, immediately following the capacity/override prose at line ~938):

> [CORE-GATE-1] A workbench MAY mark a column as requiring a named structured item to be resolved before a card enters it.
> [CORE-GATE-2] A tool MUST refuse a move into a column so marked, reporting the refusal name `unresolved-item`, while the card carries the item that column names and that item is not resolved.

and §5.9's own gloss: *"the item named is one the card itself carries, per section 5.9"*, i.e. the profile's own text already resolves the ambiguity the card description raises: "the item that column names" is not an item identified by ID from the column's declaration, it is any item whose own `column:` field names this column. The mechanism is symmetric with the trap discussed below: naming is entirely on the item's side; the column only says whether it enforces what gets named at it.

Both statements sit on the `dev` channel (§2.1: *"nothing below binds"*), were introduced at 0.10 (2026-09-03, changelog entry read in full), and `internal/profile/conformance_test.go`'s `outOfReach` table (lines 57-58) confirms neither is implemented: `"marking a column as requiring an item resolved is a definition surface v0 neither writes nor validates"` / `"v0 declares no such column, so no move can be refused for entering one"`. This card is not overdue on a binding obligation; it is closing a gap the project's own draft already named.

**This spec implements CORE-GATE-1/CORE-GATE-2 as written, with one load-bearing structural consequence for the profile document itself** (see "Profile document amendment," below) that is not optional cleanup: §6.4's own ordered-check table has to grow a row, or the refusal name CORE-GATE-2 fixes cannot legally be reused by Dinah's own test suite. That finding, not a stylistic preference, is what turns "should the profile be amended" into "the profile must be amended for this card's own tests to pass."

## The design decisions (mine, not the operator's)

**D1. A gate refuses entry to the column it names, not departure from the one before it.** CORE-GATE-2's text is unambiguous ("refuse a move into a column so marked"); the card's worry that this board had it backwards in writing is about *which column to name* when authoring a gate (name the column after the one that answers an item, never the answering column itself, see D8), not about which of the two readings the mechanism itself implements.

**D2. Declaration is a per-column boolean, not a per-column list of item kinds.** A column gets one new frontmatter field, `gate_items: true` (absent or `false` = no gating, matching every other optional column flag's default). It does **not** enumerate which item kinds it holds. Kind-selectivity is achieved entirely by which items a workbench's own filing convention writes `column:` on: a method that wants only its acceptance criteria held there simply never sets `column: Merge` on a decision or an open question, and the tool never inspects `Kind` to decide whether to gate. This is the strongest reading of "no kind of item may be special-cased in the tool"; the alternative (a per-column list of held kinds) would still be kind-neutral in the sense the ruling asked for, but it adds a second axis of configuration the profile's own text does not ask for and the item-filing convention alone already supplies for free. Reused field: `item.Column` (`bench.ItemColumnField`), unchanged.

**D3. The refusal names the item, not a count.** Matches `claimableItems`'s own existing convention (`blocking[0].ID`). Where several items on a card name the same gated column, the refusal names the first, in the same `ListIDs` order `BlockingItems` already reads.

**D4. The hold can be overridden, by the operator only, through the existing override marker.** `req.Override` already exists and is already operator-gated (`canRoute`, `mutate.go:339`) for the capacity and loop-limit rows. Extending the same marker to also lift a gate refusal needs no new flag, no new refusal name, and no new witnessed-event shape: it folds into the `moved` event's existing `override: true` flag exactly as the capacity and loop-limit overrides already do. `docs/design/format.md:847`'s documentation of that flag needs one clause added (see file list).

**D5. Interaction with the existing claim-time refusal (CORE-CLAIM-9) is additive, not a duplicate.** The two check different verbs: claim's `UnresolvedItem` blocks taking the card up *at all, from any column, for a `decision`/`open_question` alone*; the new move-time gate blocks *entering one named column, for any kind including `acceptance_criterion`*. A workbench that gates a column on a kind pair already covered by claim's blanket rule is not wasting effort, since claim only stops the card being claimed, never stops it moving unclaimed through every column in the flow (nothing in `canLand` today reads item state at all), so a workbench that cares which *column* a `decision` blocks, not merely whether the card can ever be picked up, still needs the gate. The pairing this mechanism was actually reopened for is the one claim structurally cannot reach: an `acceptance_criterion`, which claim exempts unconditionally.

**D6. Route-skip: already possible today, not deferred to a future lanes card.** The card's framing (both here and on dinah-449) treats "a hold naming a column a card's route never reaches" as a lanes-only concern. It is not. Nothing in `canMove`/`canRoute` restricts a move's destination to the flow's *next* column: `canRoute` (`mutate.go:330-346`) resolves any column the workbench declares and checks only ownership, override authority and departure legality, not adjacency. A single-route, lane-free board (which is what dinah-449 commits this project to) can still move a card directly from column 3 to column 7, skipping every gated column in between. That is not a defect this card introduces or must guard against: a gate that names a column nobody enters simply never fires, exactly as any other queue or inspection point is silently skipped by a jump-move today. I record this as a correction to the card's own premise rather than as new scope: no code is needed to handle "a route that never reaches the gated column," because reaching it was never guaranteed by anything else in the contract either.

**D7. Evaluation order.** The new row is checked in `canLand` after the capacity block and before the loop-limit block, which is where the ninth published row sits. The operator ruled on the placement in OQ-2 on 2026-09-10: the published numbering decides the evaluation order rather than the other way round, so the code moves and the page is not renumbered. An at-capacity **and** unresolved-item destination still reports `at-capacity` first, on `check.move.9`'s own precedent of resolving row-order collisions by whichever row prints first, and a regressive move that fails both this row and the departure's own `loop_limit` reports `unresolved-item`, which is the earlier of the two.

**D8. The wrong-gate ("assigns work to the step it holds") trap is real and, on the evidence read above, not mechanically detectable, and I say so rather than implying detection is coming.** The trap requires two facts to both be true of one item: (a) it names a column via `item.Column`, and (b) that column is understood, by the workbench's own unwritten convention, to be where the item gets resolved. Dinah's core has exactly one field for "the column an item names" and no field at all for "which step resolves this item": `ItemOwnerField` is free text describing *who*, never *where*, and resolution itself (D above, "How an item gets resolved") is unrestricted by column or claim, so nothing prevents an item from being resolved long before the card reaches the column it names. Because the tool cannot distinguish "this item names its own gate column because that is deliberately where the discipline lives" (the ordinary, intended use, an acceptance criterion verified during Test, naming Merge as its gate) from "this item names its own gate column because that is, by unwritten convention, also where somebody was going to answer it" (the deadlock), a structural refusal at file time would refuse the first case as often as the second. That is exactly the "refusal any value satisfies is not a refusal" trap's mirror image, a refusal *nothing* legitimate can satisfy, and the workbench instructions' own standard for a check ("say what a false failure would look like and why it cannot happen") is failed by construction here. **No code addresses this.** The mitigation is documentation, in whichever workbench adopts gating, of the same discipline this project's own working agreement already states for Andoneer's `gate_column` (name the column *after* the one that answers the item, never the one that does). I recommend, but do not require as part of this card, that a future workbench-authoring pass for this project's own board (the tracker half of dinah-449) carry that same sentence into its own method text.

## What ships

### 1. `internal/bench` - the declaration and the query

- `Column` (`bench.go:156-210`) gains `GateItems bool`.
- `readColumnIn` (`bench.go:1780-1840`) parses `gate_items` with the **strict** true/false discipline `awaiting_outside` already uses (empty -> false; `"true"` -> true; `"false"` -> false; anything else -> `contract.Malformed`), not `wip_limit`'s numeric parse and not `operator_owned`'s lenient `== "true"`.
- `knownColumnKeys` (`interchange.go:29-31`) gains `"gate_items": true`.
- `exportColumn` (`interchange.go:66-`) emits `gate_items: true` only when set, mirroring the existing `operator_owned` block immediately below it.
- `item.go`'s `ItemBlocksClaim` (`entity.go:914-936`) has its four-state switch (`resolved`/`verified`/`failed` -> not blocking, else -> blocking) extracted into a new, kind-agnostic `ItemIsResolved(item *Item) bool`. `ItemBlocksClaim` becomes a thin kind filter over it (`item.Kind == "open_question" || item.Kind == "decision"`, then `!ItemIsResolved(item)`); its exported behaviour is unchanged.
- The gate reads a state predicate of its own, `ItemLiftsColumnHold(item *Item) bool`, which admits `resolved` and `verified` alone. The operator ruled in OQ-5 on 2026-09-10 that a `failed` item goes on holding the card: the state records that somebody checked the work and it did not hold, so releasing the gate on it would open the column on the one state saying the work is wrong. Permission to proceed anyway becomes a fifth item state, which is filed separately and is not built here; until it exists, the operator's move-level override marker is what carries such a card through, which CORE-GATE-4 already permits. `ItemIsResolved` is left exactly as it is, because the claim refusal reads it, the claim refusal exempts acceptance criteria outright, and narrowing the shared predicate would have moved behaviour nobody ruled on.
- A new `GatingItems(cardDir, columnID string) []*Item` (name is Implement's to bikeshed, not the shape): every item on the card whose `Column == columnID` and `!ItemLiftsColumnHold(item)`, in `ListIDs` order, **with no kind excluded**, so an `acceptance_criterion` blocks exactly as a `decision` or `open_question` does. This is the one place the operator's "no kind hardcoded" constraint is actually load-bearing in code, since `ItemBlocksClaim` right next to it *does* hardcode a kind pair, on purpose, for a different refusal.

### 2. `internal/verb` - the check

- `canLand` (`mutate.go`) gains, after the existing capacity block and before the loop-limit block: if `destination.GateItems` and `bench.GatingItems(card.Dir, destination.ID)` is non-empty, refuse `contract.UnresolvedItem` naming the first item's ID, unless `req.Override` (already operator-verified upstream in `canRoute`), in which case the move proceeds and folds into the same returned `override` bool the capacity/loop rows already produce. The placement is D7's and is the operator's OQ-2 ruling: the row is published ninth and the loop-limit row tenth, and the code checks them in the order the page publishes them.
- `checkLists[Move]` (`checks.go`) gains this row **within the profile-matched prefix**, not appended past it, see "Profile document amendment" below for why position matters, not just content. After the amendment, `checkLists[Move]` becomes: rows 1-8 unchanged; **row 9** = `{Refusal: contract.UnresolvedItem, Key: "check.move.9"}` (the new gate row, inheriting the position and hence the key `check.move.9` under `CheckKey`'s `check.<command>.<order>` composition); **row 10** = today's loop-limit row, its `Refusal`/behavior unchanged but its `Key` becoming `check.move.10` (a pure rename, not a rewrite, see locale note).
- `pullChecks` (`checks.go`) gains the equivalent row immediately after the existing capacity row (today's `check.pull.10`), becoming `check.pull.11`; today's rows 11-14 (`NotOperator`, `Locked`, `UnresolvedItem`, `BelowTier`) shift to 12-15. Unlike the loop-limit row, this one is not narrowed away for pull the way `pullCandidates`'s comment (`pull.go:160-167`) narrows loop-limit, since nothing about a pull's forward-only landing makes a gated destination unreachable, so `pullChecks` must state the row for real, not merely inherit it silently through `canLand`.
- `internal/msg/locales/*.json` (all eight: `af`, `cs`, `de`, `en`, `es`, `fil`, `hi`, `id`): `check.move.9`'s text changes from the loop-limit sentence to the gate sentence (English: something in the register of `"the destination does not require a card's own unresolved item resolved"`); a new `check.move.10` entry carries the *old* `check.move.9` text unchanged (pure copy, new key, no new translation). `check.pull.11` is new (fresh text in all eight languages, closely mirroring `check.move.9`'s new wording); `check.pull.12` through `.15` are `check.pull.11` through `.14`'s existing text under renamed keys (pure copy). Net new translation surface: two English sentences (move's and pull's gate rows, which may be worded identically) times seven non-English locales = fourteen strings; everything else is a mechanical key rename carrying its existing text forward.

### 3. Profile document amendment, required, not optional, for the reasons below

`cmd/dinah/main_test.go`'s help-table cross-check (~line 1320-1345) does two things for every verb in `verb.ContractVerbs`: it compares `checkLists[name]`'s **first** `len(wanted)` rows against `profile.Preconditions(coreProfileText)`, row for row, by refusal name; then it asserts every row **past** that prefix carries a refusal prefixed `contract.LayerPrefix` (`"dinah."`). `Move` is in `ContractVerbs`. `internal/profile/preconditions.go`'s `Preconditions` parses the fenced fixed-width table directly beneath each `### 6.N` heading in `core-profile.md`. Today that table under `### 6.4 Move` has eight rows, so anything `checkLists[Move]` carries at position 9 or later is required, by this existing test, to be `dinah.`-prefixed. CORE-GATE-2 fixes the exact string `unresolved-item`, no prefix, not negotiable, not Dinah's to rename. **The gate row can only legally occupy `check.move.9` if the profile's own §6.4 table has nine rows, not eight.** This is not a documentation nicety chosen for tidiness; it is the only way the two requirements (CORE-GATE-2's fixed name, and the existing test's dinah-prefix rule for anything past the profile's own prefix) can both hold at once.

Required edit to `docs/spec/core-profile.md`:
- Append a ninth row to §6.4's fenced table, phrased in that table's own register, e.g. `9  the destination's own gate, if any, names no unresolved item the card carries   unresolved-item`.
- Retire `CORE-JSON-9` and reissue as **`CORE-JSON-10`** (next free number in that family, checked: `CORE-JSON-1` through `-9` are the only ones currently published), adding `gate_items` to the blessed column-object member list, on the same terms `capacity` already carries `wip_limit`'s interchange name (`CORE-JSON-9`'s own text: *"A column object MAY carry the members `instructions`, `operator_owned`, `capacity`, and `slug`."*). The document's own §2.2 rule (`DOC-VER-4`) forbids editing that list in place; a MAY-statement's member enumeration changes by retire-and-reissue, the same pattern the 0.9 changelog entry already used for `CORE-JSON-6`.
- Remove the two now-false `outOfReach` entries in `internal/profile/conformance_test.go` (lines 57-58) once the gate is implemented, and let the conformance tests exercise it for real rather than documenting it as unreached.
- Add a `### 0.13, channel dev` changelog entry mirroring the 0.10/0.11 entries already in the document (same section, read in full): identifiers affected `CORE-JSON-9` (retired), `CORE-JSON-10` (introduced); consequence-for-a-caller paragraph; and a note that this revision moves the minor number under `DOC-ORDER-2` as well, since it changes "the order in which section 6 states its checks" by growing §6.4's table, not only its extracted-statement list.
- The `docs/design/format.md` paragraph documenting the `moved` event's `override` flag (line 847) gains one clause: `"...which is a CORE-MOVE-9 capacity override, the departure column's own loop_limit, **or the destination column's own gate,**..."`.

### 4. Two pieces of stale documentation, found while reading, flagged rather than folded into scope

- `docs/design/format.md`'s "Checklist items" section still reads *"Nothing today creates or resolves an item... exercising this refusal, or clearing it, means hand-editing an item's `state` key directly."* This predates dinah-206/435's write side and is already wrong independent of this card. Worth a one-paragraph correction while a future pass is in that section of the document for any reason, but it is not this card's contract and I have not sized it as one.
- `internal/msg/locales/en.json`'s `refusal.unresolved-item.next` text (`"...names no command, because nothing in Dinah writes a checklist item yet and the edit is made in the file"`) is the same staleness, in the help catalog rather than the design doc. Same disposition: noted, not adopted into this card's acceptance criteria, since neither correction touches gating and folding them in here would be exactly the kind of scope drift the workbench instructions ask a spec to resist rather than absorb by convenience. Whoever picks this up: a comment on the card names both locations.

## Out of scope, restated

No board UI. No route/lane model (dinah-209 stays filed). No new CLI verb for declaring a gate: `gate_items`, like `wip_limit`, `loop_limit`, `operator_owned` and `awaiting_outside` before it, is a hand-edited column.md key with no `dinah column set` counterpart (`format.md`'s own text: *"operator_owned and awaiting_outside are not settable at creation; write them into the column's own file by hand, as before"*, and the same posture extends to this key). No change to `ItemBlocksClaim`'s existing kind exemption, which this card leaves exactly as CORE-CLAIM-9/0.11's changelog entry describes it.

The fifth item state that carries permission to proceed past a failed criterion is dinah-472's, and resolving a `file --column` reference to a column identifier at write time is dinah-473's. Both were ruled on here and neither is built here.
