A diagnostic proposed on 2026-08-24 says that counting the work a completed card created, split into cards it spawned and times it was sent back, distinguishes a board that is decomposing work from one that is grinding it. Many send-backs and few spawns is supposed to mean one card was standing in for several.

This board has the history to test it. Count both numbers for every card that reached Done in the past month, from the journal rather than by memory, then rank the cards by send-backs and ask independently whether the top of that list is visibly under-decomposed.

The impression that prompted this is that the three most expensive cards of 2026-08-24 each took between four and seven returns, spawned almost nothing, and each carried several separable pieces of work. If the ranking agrees with a reading of the cards themselves, the diagnostic is real and cheap. If the correlation is weak, the count is measuring review thoroughness rather than card size, which is worth knowing before anybody builds a report on it.

Record the counterexamples either way. A card that was returned many times and was genuinely one piece of work says something about where the returns came from instead.
