Every carried card's checklist crossed with it, and the items Dinah has no state for are parked where a command can find them again. For each open card that carried checklist items on Andoneer at the cutover instant, the Dinah card carries an item per Andoneer item in one of Dinah's four states (pending, resolved, verified, failed), matching on kind, on the column the item names, on the owner, and on the settled-or-pending state. The comparison runs over every such card and prints how many cards it compared and how many items in total, so a comparison over zero fails rather than passing. Four checks inside it. First, an item that named a gate column on Andoneer names the corresponding Dinah column identifier, resolved to identifiers on both sides rather than compared by name. Second, an item resolved on Andoneer is resolved on Dinah with its note carried, and one that was pending is pending, because a resolved item crossing pending silently holds its card and a pending one crossing resolved silently removes a stop somebody filed on purpose. Third, every Andoneer item in the `obsolete` state is accounted for: none exists as a Dinah checklist item, and each appears in the carried card's body under the literal heading `## Retired checklist items (no Dinah state for these yet)` with its Andoneer kind, local reference, state and note; the run prints how many obsolete items it found on the Andoneer side and how many body entries it matched them to, and the two numbers are equal. Fourth, the parked items are findable without anybody remembering they exist, and the census is ANCHORED rather than a plain text search: `grep -rlE '^## Retired checklist items \(no Dinah state for these yet\)$' <workbench>/cards/*/card.md` run against the real workbench enumerates every card carrying them, and the run prints that list and its length, which must equal the number of cards the third check parked items on. A plain `grep -rl "Retired checklist items"` does not satisfy this check and fails it, because it also matches a card that merely mentions the phrase in its prose. A failing run is any mismatch in kind, column, owner, state or note, any obsolete item present as a checklist item, any obsolete item with no body entry, a heading that is not that literal, an unanchored census, an enumeration whose length disagrees, or a compared-card count of zero.
