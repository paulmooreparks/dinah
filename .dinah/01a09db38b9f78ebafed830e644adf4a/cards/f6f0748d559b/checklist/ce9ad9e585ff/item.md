note: "1.101 is the first stable API version declaring `registerMcpServerDefinitionProvider`, determined by the unpkg sweep recorded in the spec and reproduced at review. Keeping `^1.90.0` and feature-detecting would ship a manifest contribution point and a call that an older editor cannot honour, and how an older editor treats an unknown contribution point is documented nowhere, so the version floor is the documented route and the feature detection is the measured one.\n\nWHAT THE FLOOR COSTS, added at Spec round 2 because round 1 recorded the technical reason and not the price. The manifest today declares `\"vscode\": \"^1.90.0\"`, so every reader on VS Code 1.90 or later can install the extension and receives each new version as it is published. Raising the floor to `^1.101.0` ends that for everyone between 1.90 and 1.100. They do not lose the copy they already have and nothing breaks for them, but the Marketplace stops offering them new versions, and that is every future version rather than only this one, so a fix to the tree or the status bar that has nothing to do with MCP will not reach them either. They will not be told; an extension simply stops updating. The remedy available to such a reader is to update VS Code, which costs nothing and is what most readers do anyway. The reader it genuinely strands is the one who cannot, which is the person on a managed installation somebody else pins, or on a remote or Codespaces host whose server version is not theirs to choose. Nobody has counted how many readers that is, and this project has no telemetry that could.\n\nThe alternative that would avoid the cost is publishing two builds from one source, an MCP-capable one at the new floor and a maintenance one at the old, and it is rejected here as out of proportion to a single-seat companion extension. It is recorded so that the operator sees the trade rather than half of it, since this decision is his to accept at Operator Design Review. Activation is untouched: `manifest.test.ts:214-222` asserts the list is exactly the two existing events, and that assertion stands. The honest consequence is that a reader whose workbench is reached by the ancestor walk rather than by a `workbench.md` inside the workspace folder gets no registration until something else activates the extension. That is the existing activation contract's limitation and it is stated in the spec rather than papered over."

`engines.vscode` and `@types/vscode` move to `^1.101.0`, and no activation event is added.
